AI for Smart Cities of Tomorrow

Robotaxis on the streets.

Drones in the air.

How do we test transport policy before it reaches the street?

The gap

Rule-based agents can’t anticipate the unfamiliar.

Conventional traveller agents follow predefined rules and cannot capture adaptive decisions under unfamiliar technologies and policies.

This project replaces them with AI residents: LLM-based agents that reason and adapt like real travellers.

Source: joint proposal (A*STAR/RGC 1, Sep 2026), Part II §1 Scientific Abstract, §4 Problem Statement. Agent motion is illustrative, not a result.
The approach

Simulate, diagnose, self-correct.

AI residents are embedded in the existing HKSim and Singapore island-wide simulators. Calibration agents diagnose behavioural discrepancies that affect policy estimates, correct them and re-evaluate the simulation.

Expected outcomes
  1. 1
    A scalable agentic simulation systemBalanced cost and fidelity: scale, computational cost and behavioural fidelity quantified.
  2. 2
    An agentic calibration systemDiscrepancy discovery and self-improvement: diagnosis, targeted alignment and re-evaluation in a loop.
  3. 3
    An independent policy evaluation benchmarkRobotaxi and UAV cases in Hong Kong and Singapore. Models frozen before testing on held-out evidence.
Source: joint proposal (A*STAR/RGC 1, Sep 2026), Part II §1 Scientific Abstract, §3 Objectives and Key Results.
Why now

Phase 1 coincides with deployment decisions.

Public autonomous shuttle rides began in Punggol on 1 April 2026, and Waymo plans a commercial robotaxi service in Singapore in 2028. Phase 1 would deliver validated evidence as those services scale.
